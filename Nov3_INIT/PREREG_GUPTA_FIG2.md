## Hamiltonian as parsed from the TeX (Sec. "Quantum Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model", lines 138–142)

```
H = -\sum_{1\le i<j\le L} J_{ij} Z_i Z_j - \Gamma \sum_{i=1}^{L} X_i
J_ij Gaussian, zero mean, variance \mathcal{J}/N, \mathcal{J} = 1
```
Fig. 2 caption: "exact diagonalization results for the ground state energy and the gap Δ = E1 − E0 (inset) … Γ = 1 … averaged over 30 instances". The y-axis is \(E_{GS}/L\).

Parsed convention:
- **Pauli** operators (Z, X), not spin-1/2 \(S=\sigma/2\).
- **Single** sum over i<j (each pair once).
- The coupling sign is \(-J_{ij}\). Because the distribution is symmetric about 0, the sign does not change the ensemble.
- The field term is \(-\Gamma\sum X\) with Γ=+1. There is no longitudinal field.
- The variance is \(\mathcal J/N\) with \(\mathcal J=1\). In the SK section N is not separately defined; it is taken as N = L = 12, the number of spins. N is the Majorana count only in the SYK section.

**Primary convention (frozen; not changed after results):** \(H=-\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}Z_iZ_j-\sum_iX_i\) with \(J_{ij}\sim\mathcal N(0,1/12)\), i.e. std \(1/\sqrt{12}\). This is generated by the existing repo `make_J(seed, 12)` (`default_rng(seed).normal(0, 1/sqrt(N))` over the upper triangle in row-major order) and then **negated** to match the paper's \(-J\) sign. Negation leaves the ensemble identical, but it is applied literally anyway.

Secondary convention probes on the same 30 instances are reported only as sensitivity checks and never replace the primary:
- (S1) spin-1/2: \(H=-\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}S^z_iS^z_j-\Gamma\sum S^x_i\) = \(-\tfrac14\sum JZZ-\tfrac12\sum X\).
- (S2) double sum over \(i\ne j\), i.e. 2J on each pair.
- (S3) variance \(\mathcal J^2/N\) — identical to the primary at \(\mathcal J=1\), so it is listed and not recomputed.

## Computation (per instance)

1. **Independent construction A:** a dense 4096×4096 matrix from Kronecker products of 2×2 Pauli matrices, then full `numpy.linalg.eigh`, giving all eigenvalues and eigenvectors.
2. **Construction B:** a bitwise sparse matrix, parity-block (P=±) `eigsh(SA, k=8, tol=1e-12)`, plus a full-space `eigsh k=8`.
3. Report \(E_0/L\), the full gap \(E_1-E_0\), and the parity \(\langle P\rangle\) of the ground state and of the first excited state, with \(P=\prod X_i\) (resolved inside degenerate clusters with tol 1e-9). Report \(\Delta_+=E_1^+-E_0^+\) (the same/even-sector gap), \(\Delta_-\), and the cross split \(E_0^--E_0^+\). Report the coupling \(c_1=|\langle1_+|\sum X_i|0\rangle|\) and the lowest same-sector coupled gap at τ=1e-8. Report residuals \(\|Hv-Ev\|\), \(\|[H,P]\|\), and the agreements A vs B.

## Comparison and decision rule (frozen)

The quantities compared are E/L and the gap. For each, compute: our mean, SD, SEM and t-95% CI (n=30); the Welch 95% CI for (ours − Fig. 2) using the extracted Fig. 2 values; and a two-sample KS p-value as a secondary check. Both samples are draws of 30 from the same nominal ensemble, so their means should differ by about \(\sqrt2\)·SEM purely from sampling.

- **AGREE** for a quantity: the Welch 95% CI of the mean difference includes 0.
- **TENSION/DISAGREE** for a quantity: the CI excludes 0. This is reported as statistical tension with stated assumptions, not as an accusation; the unknown seeds, generator and gap definition are listed as possible causes.
- **Gap definition:** the primary comparison uses the full gap \(E_1-E_0\), which is literally what the caption says. \(\Delta_+\) is also compared. If only one of the two definitions agrees, that is reported. If both or neither agree, the gap definition is reported as **not resolved** by this test.
- Each quantity gets its own verdict. There is no multiplicity correction, and that is stated.
