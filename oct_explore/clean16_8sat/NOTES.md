## Methods, code and deviations (8-SAT)

Code: `oct_explore/clean16_8sat/` (`common.py`, `qaoa.py`, `gcost.py`, `wsat.py`, `paper_angles.py`, `report.py`); raw per-formula p in `oct_explore/results/clean16_8sat/*.json`, logs in `oct_explore/logs/clean16_8sat/`.

- **Instance filter (checked against the paper text).** Paper §4.2: "sample up to 10000 instances from CNF(n,k,r) ... and retain only satisfiable instances"; Def. 1: m ~ Poisson(rn), k literals uniformly *with replacement*; Table 1: r(8) = 176.54. We do exactly this for **all** train/val/test sets: keep a formula iff its exact energy table has min 0 (≥1 solution); **no** unique-solution filter (typical nsol 1–3). Tautological clauses (x ∨ ¬x) are never violated; repeated literals collapse.
- **QAOA L4** = clean16 `qaoa.py` (fixed.py `--proj full --mixer gray`), only change: nm = 1/r instead of n/m (m now varies per formula). 1000 train formulas per n, L-BFGS 12 starts, 3 seeds.
- **Global cost L4** = clean16 `gcost.py` (G16_full_w recipe, x0 = fresh QAOA winner, from scratch). Changes, identical math: clauses zero-padded to a fixed M(n) = ceil(rn + 8√(rn)) for batching; the dense (2^n,B,m,k) literal tensor (infeasible at m≈3500, k=8) is replaced by exact sparse (clause, violating-string) lists with segment_sum (checked: bincount of the list == energy table for every formula); E_w = nm·(g_l·E + Σ_j w_jl viol_j).
- **Native ckpt_110: not run.** The OpenEvolve program and the hash-locked frozen circuit hard-code 4-literal clauses (V/SG (F,m,4), 15 literal subsets, (m,4) weight tables). Running it at k=8 would require rewriting the program, i.e. it would no longer be "what OpenEvolve found".
- **WalkSATlm** = `qaoa_ksat/walksatlm_c/wslm.c` k=8, p=0.30, w1=6, w2=5, n-flip tries with restarts, 500 runs/formula; lookups = tries + flips; the paper's unit (tries) is also reported.
- **Reference rows**: the paper's published fixed angles (PhaseCraft qaoa_ksat_paper_data, key (8,176.54)), evaluated untrained on the same test formulas with exp(+iγE/2), RX(β).
- Ideal one-bit-flip mixer (= qubit RX) for every quantum method.

### Native rows for 8-SAT (added 2026-10-22 21:45 CST)
- **Why no faithful ckpt_110 port.** The frozen `circuit_p` is generic in the number of literals, but it builds the dense violation tensor (2^n × entries × m). At k=8, m≈3500, and with R=4 placements that is ≈15 GB per n=20 formula, so it cannot run here. The OpenEvolve program is also k=4-specific in its core: `geom` enumerates the 15 literal subsets (255 at k=8, giving (F,R,m,255,8) tensors), and the clause-head shapes, `local_tables` and `wc` are (…,4). Porting it means redesigning the program and the frozen simulator, so it would no longer be "what OpenEvolve found". It was therefore not run.
- **Native-projected cost L4, QAOA-style** (`qaoa.py --proj chain`, tag QC): the clause cost nm·γ_l·E is least-squares projected (uniform measure) onto the chain's native support: per-cavity SNAP terms plus neighbour-pair bus terms, with every other Walsh term dropped. Mixer: ideal one-bit-flip. 8 shared angles, block assignment, same training as QAOA L4 (1000/n, 12 L-BFGS starts, 3 seeds).
- **Native-projected cost L4 + GNN clause weights** (`gcost.py --proj chain`, tag GC): the G16_full_w recipe with the same chain projection applied to the GNN-weighted cost. This is the clean16 native-GNN analogue. x0 = the fresh full-QAOA winner, 3 seeds. The ckpt_110 Grover/rank dS/dB families are **not** included, because they rely on the k=4 tables above.

### Why the native-projected cost scales as ~2.1^n at k=8 (`projloss.py`, 20 test formulas per n, block assignment)
| n | cavities | clauses in 1 cavity or a neighbour pair | var(E) kept by projection | corr(projected E, E) | P(argmin of projected E is a solution) | random guess 2^n/nsol (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 2 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.00 | 256 |
| 12 | 3 | 0.078 | 0.514 | 0.716 | 0.00 | 4.1e+03 |
| 16 | 4 | 0.012 | 0.330 | 0.574 | 0.05 | 6.55e+04 |
| 20 | 5 | 0.003 | 0.238 | 0.487 | 0.00 | 5.24e+05 |

The SNAP and neighbour-bus gates can represent only Walsh terms that sit inside one cavity or inside a neighbour pair. An 8-literal clause touches ~6–8 distinct variables, so from n=12 on it almost always spans non-adjacent cavities. Only 7.8% / 1.2% / 0.3% of clauses are exactly representable at n=12/16/20, and what the projection keeps is mainly their low-order part. The projected cost keeps 51% → 24% of the variance of the true cost, and its correlation with the true cost drops from 0.72 to 0.49. Its minimum essentially never lands on a solution: the argmin of the projected E is a solution in ≤5% of formulas. The circuit therefore follows a smoothed surrogate whose optima are *not* the solutions. Its cost grows faster than blind guessing (b≈2.1 vs ≈2 for 2^n/nsol), yet it stays below the random-guess level of 2^n/nsol over n≤20 because at n=8 (2 cavities) the projection is exact (fraction 1.0, corr 1.0). At k=4 the same projection keeps far more, because 4-literal clauses span ≤2 cavities much more often, which is why native ckpt_110 was competitive in the 4-SAT table.

### L=14 QAOA and L=14 Global Cost on 8-SAT (added 2026-10-23)
- **L=14 QAOA baseline in table = Paper's published p=14 angles (`P14.json`).** The paper's p=14 angles (optimised by the authors on 100 n=12 instances to maximise mean $p_{\text{succ}}$) scale better to n=16,20 ($1.209^n$ median / $1.256^n$ B90; n=20 med/B90 = 26.0/131) than our n=8+12 L-BFGS log-loss QAOA L14 (`Q14_s0..s2`, $1.233^n$ median / $1.304^n$ B90; n=20 med/B90 = 25.3/167, which overfits the B90 tail to n=8,12), so we report only the stronger paper p=14 QAOA row in the main table (raw `Q14_s*.json` files remain saved in `results/clean16_8sat/`).
- **Global cost L14** (`gcost.py --L 14 --proj full --x0 results/clean16_8sat/Q14_s2.json`, tag `G14`): 14-layer all-to-all global cost with GNN clause weights $w_{jl}$ (`Wc` shape $(64,14)$) and per-formula pooled $(\beta_l, \gamma_l)$ deltas (`G2` shape $(64,88)$) across all 14 layers (89,496 parameters total). Initialised at step 0 with `--x0` = `Q14_s2` (best L=14 QAOA on val n=12), trained from scratch for 3000 Adam steps on 5000-formula pools (n=8+12, base 61e9), 3 seeds, early-stopped and seed-selected on val n=12 (base 63e9), evaluated on unseen test n=8,12,16,20 (base 62e9). Ideal one-bit-flip mixer throughout.
