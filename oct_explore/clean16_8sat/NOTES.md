## Methods, code and deviations (8-SAT)

Code: `oct_explore/clean16_8sat/` (`common.py`, `qaoa.py`, `gcost.py`, `native.py` + `native_prog.py`, `wsat.py`, `paper_angles.py`, `report.py`); raw per-formula p in `oct_explore/results/clean16_8sat/*.json`, logs in `oct_explore/logs/clean16_8sat/`.

- **Instance filter (checked against the paper text).** Paper §4.2: "sample up to 10000 instances from CNF(n,k,r) ... and retain only satisfiable instances"; Def. 1: m ~ Poisson(rn), k literals uniformly *with replacement*; Table 1: r(8) = 176.54. We do exactly this for **all** train/val/test sets: keep a formula iff its exact energy table has min 0 (≥1 solution); **no** unique-solution filter (typical nsol 1–3). Tautological clauses (x ∨ ¬x) are never violated; repeated literals collapse.
- **QAOA L4** = clean16 `qaoa.py` (fixed.py `--proj full --mixer gray`), only change: nm = 1/r instead of n/m (m now varies per formula). 1000 train formulas per n, L-BFGS 12 starts, 3 seeds.
- **Global cost L4** = clean16 `gcost.py` (G16_full_w recipe, x0 = fresh QAOA winner, from scratch). Changes, identical math: clauses zero-padded to a fixed M(n) = ceil(rn + 8√(rn)) for batching; the dense (2^n,B,m,k) literal tensor (infeasible at m≈3500, k=8) is replaced by exact sparse (clause, violating-string) lists with segment_sum (checked: bincount of the list == energy table for every formula); E_w = nm·(g_l·E + Σ_j w_jl viol_j).
- **Native `ckpt_110` (shots, round-robin)** = clean16 `native.py` + `native_prog.py` (= net16r `ckpt_110` `best_program.py`), ported to k=8 (`oct_explore/clean16_8sat/native.py`, `native_prog.py`, tag `N_s0..s2`). Uses the exact same GNN + `corr` + `grover` (`dS`/`dB`) architecture, training recipe (`OE_TRAIN_N=8,12`, 600 formulas per n, 180 s budget, 3 seeds), R=4 variable-to-cavity placements (min-cut + 3 seeded random), and round-robin evaluation (`1 / mean_r p_r`) as the 4-SAT table, with minimal k=8 adaptations listed below.
- **WalkSATlm** = `qaoa_ksat/walksatlm_c/wslm.c` k=8, p=0.30, w1=6, w2=5, n-flip tries with restarts, 500 runs/formula; lookups = tries + flips; the paper's unit (tries) is also reported.
- **Reference rows**: the paper's published fixed angles (PhaseCraft qaoa_ksat_paper_data, key (8,176.54)), evaluated untrained on the same test formulas with exp(+iγE/2), RX(β).
- Ideal one-bit-flip mixer (= qubit RX) for every quantum method.

### Native `ckpt_110` k=8 adaptations (updated 2026-10-23)
To run the 4-SAT `ckpt_110` program (`clean16/native_prog.py`) and simulator (`clean16/native.py`) on the paper's 8-SAT ensemble without changing the network architecture or gate support:
1. **Sparse `circuit_p` (`native.py:circuit_p_sparse`).** The frozen `circuit_p` builds a dense `(2^n, B, m, k)` literal-violation tensor (~15 GB per n=20 formula at m≈3531, k=8, R=4). As in `clean16_8sat/gcost.py`, `circuit_p_sparse` replaces that dense contraction with exact sparse `(clause j, violating string x)` lists and `jax.ops.segment_sum`, then permutes `Ew` into chain order for each of the R=4 placements before applying the identical `project_product_j` + `dS` + `dB` + ideal mixer layers (verified to match `frozen_circuit.circuit_p` to `< 10^{-9}` forward and backward on n=8, 12). At n=20, formulas are built and scored one at a time to bound peak RAM.
2. **Exact Walsh simulated annealing for min-cut placement (`native.py:anneal_fast`).** `assign.anneal` enumerates `2^k - 1` subsets per clause in Python, which is slow at m≈3500, k=8 and does not collapse repeated literals. `anneal_fast` computes the exact Walsh spectrum `W = fwht(E)` once and evaluates kept chain variance by inclusion-exclusion over the 16 submasks of each 4-bit cavity (`C-1` adjacent pairs minus `C-2` interior cavities = `256(C-1) - 16(C-2)` lookups per swap step, bit-for-bit identical to `assign.anneal` on 4-SAT).
3. **Closed-form `geom` (`native_prog.py:geom`).** Instead of enumerating a `(F, R, m, 2^k - 1, k)` boolean subset table (`SUB`, 15 subsets at k=4 vs 255 at k=8), `geom` counts literals per cavity `n_c` and computes the exact number of non-empty subsets living in one cavity or an adjacent cavity pair via `rcnt = Σ_p (2^{n_p + n_{p+1}} - 1) - Σ_{interior c} (2^{n_c} - 1)`, giving `lost = 1 - rcnt / (2^K - 1)` (verified 0.0 max diff vs `SUB` enumeration).
4. **Incidence-based `local_tables` (`native_prog.py:local_tables`).** Instead of `jax.nn.one_hot(V, n)` over literal slots, `local_tables` uses the unsigned/signed clause-variable incidence matrices `inc, sgn` (`cpos = (inc + sgn)/2`, `cneg = (inc - sgn)/2`) to test whether any in-cavity literal is satisfied at each of the 16 cavity levels (verified 0.0 max diff vs the k=4 implementation, and handles repeated literals in the paper's with-replacement ensemble).
5. **Size-free `K` normalisation and Poisson clause padding (`M(n) = ceil(rn + 8√(rn))`).** Variable-to-clause message scaling `/4.0` and out-of-scope literal fraction `o / 4.0` are generalised to `/K` and `o / K` (`K = 8`), cost normalisation uses `nm = 1/r` (`CM.NM`), and padded clause slots (`SG == 0`) are masked out via the real-clause mask `cm` so they contribute zero to degrees, `geom`, `corr`, `local_tables`, and `w`. Minibatch sizes during the 180 s training loop are `bs = {8: 4, 12: 2}` to fit `m ≈ 1412..2118` in memory.
6. *(Retired from main table)* Earlier native-projected baselines (`qaoa.py --proj chain` -> `QC_s*.json`, and `gcost.py --proj chain` -> `GC_s*.json`) remain saved in `oct_explore/results/clean16_8sat/` for reference.

### Why native chain circuits scale as ~1.99^n–2.15^n at k=8 (`projloss.py`, 20 test formulas per n, block assignment)
| n | cavities | clauses in 1 cavity or a neighbour pair | var(E) kept by projection | corr(projected E, E) | P(argmin of projected E is a solution) | random guess 2^n/nsol (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 2 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.00 | 256 |
| 12 | 3 | 0.078 | 0.514 | 0.716 | 0.00 | 4.1e+03 |
| 16 | 4 | 0.012 | 0.330 | 0.574 | 0.05 | 6.55e+04 |
| 20 | 5 | 0.003 | 0.238 | 0.487 | 0.00 | 5.24e+05 |

The SNAP and neighbour-bus gates can represent only Walsh terms that sit inside one cavity or inside a neighbour pair. An 8-literal clause touches ~6–8 distinct variables, so from n=12 on it almost always spans non-adjacent cavities. Only 7.8% / 1.2% / 0.3% of clauses are exactly representable at n=12/16/20, and what the projection keeps is mainly their low-order part. The projected cost keeps 51% → 24% of the variance of the true cost, and its correlation with the true cost drops from 0.72 to 0.49. Its minimum essentially never lands on a solution: the argmin of the projected E is a solution in ≤5% of formulas. Even with `ckpt_110`'s min-cut + random R=4 round-robin placements and learned `dS`/`dB` phase tables (which improve n=20 median cost from ~82,800 for `Native-projected L4 + GNN` down to ~24,700–35,200 for `Native ckpt_110`), the 2-local cavity-chain gate support cannot capture 8-body clauses across 3–5 cavities as strongly as it captures 4-body clauses in 4-SAT.

### L=14 QAOA and L=14 Global Cost on 8-SAT (added 2026-10-23)
- **L=14 QAOA baseline in table = Paper's published p=14 angles (`P14.json`).** The paper's p=14 angles (optimised by the authors on 100 n=12 instances to maximise mean $p_{\text{succ}}$) scale better to n=16,20 ($1.209^n$ median / $1.256^n$ B90; n=20 med/B90 = 26.0/131) than our n=8+12 L-BFGS log-loss QAOA L14 (`Q14_s0..s2`, $1.233^n$ median / $1.304^n$ B90; n=20 med/B90 = 25.3/167, which overfits the B90 tail to n=8,12), so we report only the stronger paper p=14 QAOA row in the main table (raw `Q14_s*.json` files remain saved in `results/clean16_8sat/`).
- **Global cost L14** (`gcost.py --L 14 --proj full --x0 results/clean16_8sat/Q14_s2.json`, tag `G14`): 14-layer all-to-all global cost with GNN clause weights $w_{jl}$ (`Wc` shape $(64,14)$) and per-formula pooled $(\beta_l, \gamma_l)$ deltas (`G2` shape $(64,88)$) across all 14 layers (89,496 parameters total). Initialised at step 0 with `--x0` = `Q14_s2` (best L=14 QAOA on val n=12), trained from scratch for 3000 Adam steps on 5000-formula pools (n=8+12, base 61e9), 3 seeds, early-stopped and seed-selected on val n=12 (base 63e9), evaluated on unseen test n=8,12,16,20 (base 62e9). Ideal one-bit-flip mixer throughout.
