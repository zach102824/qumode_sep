## Probes, in priority order

### 1. Spectral mixer atom: W_H·SNAP(θ⃗)·W_H† (aims at goals a and c)

**What to try.** Your current W only constrains its first column, W|0⟩=|flat⟩. That's all PROJ needs, but it means a phase on any other level in the W frame has no meaning. Instead, compile W_H = Π_gray·H^⊗3, a full unitary on the 8 computational levels. Then use shell phases θ_k = φ_{popcount(k)} with φ₁, φ₂, φ₃ shared per layer.
- If the phases rise linearly with popcount, the layer is exactly exp(−iβΣX), the ideal 3-bit X mixer.
- A phase on level k=0 only (θ_0=β) is exactly your current PROJ, because the projector onto the flat state doesn't depend on W's other columns.
- Phases that grow nonlinearly with popcount give two- and three-bit mixing terms. You don't currently have these.

**Why it might win.**
- Your current options are a fixed mixer type (projector) or Gray-X, which needed about 8 SNAP+D pairs refitted for every β. Here W_H is calibrated once and every angle lives in SNAP phases, so it's the same hardware story as PROJ.
- It adds 3 size-independent parameters per layer.
- You can start the optimizer at exactly the champion, so it can only match or improve on training. Probe 5 can't reach this, because its frame W has undefined columns.
- An optional second frame is W_F = DFT₈. A phase of 2β·cos(2πk/8) then gives a walk around the Gray ring of Fock levels, which flips one bit per step along 8 of the 12 cube edges.

**Cheapest first experiment.**
- Use the ideal W_H matrix embedded at nf=16 (identity on levels 8–15). Don't compile yet.
- Three arms at L16:
  - (A) the champion, re-evaluated as-is.
  - (B) X-only (linear phases). This is the depth-matched apples-to-apples Gray-X control you asked for.
  - (C) three free shell phases, started at the champion.
- Train on the train split of {9, 12}: 120 steps of Adam, then L-BFGS.
- Metric: test Med/B90 at n15 and n18, plus L×Med. Promote if C beats A by at least 1.3× in Med at both n15 and n18.
- Compile W_H for real only if C wins.

### 2. Train the schedule across depths so it extrapolates (aims at goal b, target b ≤ 1.15)

**What to try.**
- First, fit q = 3–8 sine modes by least squares to the champion's 16 γ and 16 β values, and evaluate without retraining. This shows how smooth the champion is, and whether a schedule-only model can reach it.
- Then train a sine schedule with q=6 and one extra depth-scaling exponent: θ_ℓ(L) = (16/L)^α · Σ_k c_k·sin(...). Sample L ∈ {8, 16} at each step, which gives about 14 parameters that don't depend on size or depth.

**Why.**
- The champion already *is* a fixed-angle, size-independent schedule. Fourier is just a compressed form of it. What Fourier adds is depth extrapolation, and q3_Ltrain6 loses that past L16: n15 goes 6.00 → 5.99 and n9 gets worse, 2.07 → 2.2.
- Right now the deploy step keeps per-layer angles fixed, so total evolution time grows like L. Learning α lets the training data choose how angles should shrink with depth.

**Cheapest first experiment.**
- The distillation takes about 10 minutes and needs no training. Report n15/n18 test Med versus q at L16.
- Then do multi-depth training: about 150 steps, deploying at L ∈ {16, 24, 32}.
- Metric: b over n12/15/18 at L24, and whether n15 Med keeps falling past L16.

### 3. Separate cost angle per clause span (the deferred probe 4, without switching to K=4 yet)

**What to try.** Split COST into separate costs for clauses touching 2, 3 and 4 cavities, each with its own γ_ℓ^(s): 3 per layer. Start every split angle at the champion's γ_ℓ. You can compute each clause's span from `cls_v` as the number of distinct values of v//3, so the packs don't need regenerating.

**Why.** The projector only mixes within each 8-level cavity, so clauses spread across more cavities probably want a different phase. Each angle is tied to a clause class, not to an instance, so it can't memorize the way the GNN heads did. It's also the honest hardware breakdown: phases inside a cavity versus phases across cavities.

**Cheapest first experiment.**
- L16, warm-started from the champion, 120 steps on the train split of {9, 12}. Evaluate on n15/n18 test.
- Inspect the learned ratios γ^(2):γ^(3):γ^(4).
- Risk: span-4 clauses make up only 16% at n12 but 40% at n18. If transfer breaks, add n9 instances embedded into 4 cavities with 3 dummy slots, which creates span-4 clauses at small cost.

### 4. Pair projector, a cross-cavity atom (aims at goal c)

**What to try.** Add (W⊗W)·SNAP₀₀(β₂)·(W†⊗W†): a phase on the joint |0,0⟩ state of two cavities. Apply it in an even/odd brickwork over adjacent cavities, with β₂ shared per layer and started at 0.

**Why.**
- The local projector only moves amplitude within single 8-level blocks. Every clause spans at least 2 cavities, so correlated moves have to be built up over depth.
- This is a Grover-type mixer on 64-state blocks, the next rung between your local projector and a global Grover mixer.
- It's native with one transmon shared by two cavities: a number-selective pulse at the bare qubit frequency only fires when both cavities are in Fock level 0.

**Cheapest first experiment.**
- Use the ideal operator at L16, warm-started from the champion: 100 steps, then test n15/n18.
- Ablation: a single β₂ shared across all layers.
- Watch the n18/n15 ratio. If Grover-like behavior takes over, it will win at n15 and lose on scaling.
