**Before the probes: three facts from your own runs change what "dead" means.**

- **The n12 collapse comes from the GNN, not from the qumode ansatz.** The first fixed-angle run of `evolve_seq.py` uses the same `SNAP→D→COST` L5 motif with no GNN and only 80 steps. It gets n12 Med/B90 = 1728/7869. The GNN long-confirm got 4e8. Uniform guessing on your n12 pack (median nsol = 2) gives 2048/4096. So the fixed-angle motif is about as good as random guessing, and the GNN's emitted heads are about 10⁵× *worse* than random at n12. That is out-of-distribution blow-up in the heads, not physics.
- **The current motif is weak even at train n.** The same seq run gets n6 Med 44, worse than uniform (32). Uniform at n9 is 256/512. A coherent-state prep plus one SNAP+D per layer cannot reach the flat state (that needs about 3 pairs) or a Gray-X mixer (about 8 pairs). `mutate_motif` caps a layer at 6 ops with cold init, so the search space doesn't contain the known-good mixer.
- **Your transfer reference is the simplest circuit.** `oct_explore/results/CLEAN_SCALING_16LEVEL.md` uses the same 4-SAT family (m = round(9.93n)). There, plain QAOA L4 with the *ideal* Gray-digit X mixer and 8 total parameters reaches b ≈ 1.245 (n12 Med 12.8). The evolved "native" program reached b ≈ 1.40 (median) and 1.77 (B90). The ideal-X circuit is a pure bit-space circuit, so it doesn't depend on packing; you can run it on the OCT29 3-bit-per-cavity packs as the ceiling to beat.

Two structural reasons small→large fails here:

- **n6 is a degenerate regime.** It has 60 clauses over only C(6,4) = 15 possible variable sets, so every variable sits in about 40 clauses. Nothing is tree-like.
- **Packing shifts with n.** With 3 bits per cavity, the number of cavities a clause touches ("span") is distributed as follows:

| n | span 2 | span 3 | span 4 |
|---|--------|--------|--------|
| 6 | 100% | – | – |
| 9 | 36% | 64% | – |
| 12 | 18% | 65% | 16% |
| 18 | 7% | 53% | 40% |

  Any cavity-block mixer (SNAP+D acting on 8 levels) "sees" this distribution and is out of distribution at large n. A bit-product mixer does not.

### 1. Driver bake-off: the prep must be the ground state of the mixer, and β should be one native knob (highest priority)

**What to try.** Compile one n-independent flat-prep block W (about 3 SNAP+D pairs per cavity, F ≥ 0.999, leak < 1e-3), so that W|0⟩ = |flat₈⟩. Then use the per-cavity projector driver:

`|0⟩ → W → [U_Φ(γ_ℓ) → W · SNAP₀(β_ℓ) · W†]^L`

- SNAP₀(β) puts a phase only on Fock level 0, so this layer equals exp(−iβ|flat⟩⟨flat|) on each cavity.
- That gives 2 parameters per layer, shared across cavities.

Run it against two other arms under identical conditions: (a) the ideal Gray-X mixer (reference), and (b) the current single SNAP+D.

**Why it might transfer.**
- Fixed-angle transfer in QAOA comes from a discretized-annealing mechanism. That needs the prep to be the driver's ground state and a product driver with an n-independent gap. The current coherent-state prep with a generic SNAP+D satisfies neither.
- The hardware argument is stronger than for Gray-X. W is calibrated *once* for all layers and all β, and β is just a SNAP phase. Gray-X needs about 8 pairs re-fitted per β value.
- Leakage is confined to W and W†, which are calibrated offline. That matters because per-cavity leak compounds as (1−ε)^C.

**Cheapest experiment.**
- Compile W on a single cavity at nf = 16 (minutes).
- Run a linear-ramp schedule with no training: γ_ℓ = Δ·ℓ/L, β_ℓ = Δ(1−ℓ/L), L = 4, scanning only Δ at n9.
- Evaluate n9, n12, n15 (16⁵ ≈ 1M amplitudes, fine on CPU).
- Then run L-BFGS on the 8 parameters at n9 and re-evaluate n12 and n15.
- Metric: fitted b from Med and B90 over 9/12/15. Compare against uniform (2.0), ideal-X (about 1.25), and the current motif.

### 2. Change the training sizes, not the model: drop n6, train on n9+n12, zero-shot n15/n18

**What to try.** Re-run the best fixed-angle arm from probe 1 (and the current motif) with train sizes {9, 12} instead of {6, 9}, with promotion on zero-shot n15. If n12 training is too slow, use an "empty-slot embedding" instead. Put an n9 instance into C = 4 cavities with 3 free dummy bits, assigning variables to random slots. That exposes span-4 clauses at small-instance cost.

**Why.** It removes the two shifts above: the dense, non-tree-like n6 regime, and a span distribution that never contains span-4 clauses. Any cavity-aware ansatz (native SNAP blocks, projector driver, class weights) needs this. Bit-product mixers don't, which is itself a useful diagnostic.

**Cheapest experiment.** About 200 Adam or L-BFGS steps on 2L = 8 parameters, train {9, 12}, B = 32, nf = 12–16. Metric: the ratio Med15/Med12 and B90_15/B90_12, compared with the same model trained on {6, 9}.

### 3. Parameterize the schedule as a function so depth can grow at deployment

**What to try.** Use γ(t) and β(t) as q = 2–3 Fourier modes (the Zhou et al. FOURIER parameterization), sampled at L points. Train at L = 4 and n9/n12. At n15/n18, evaluate L ∈ {4, 6, 8, 12} with *no retraining*. Optionally add an INTERP warm start from L to L+1.

**Why.** Boulebnane–Montanaro shows the exponent at fixed p is n-independent and drops as p grows. A schedule function lets you buy a better exponent at large n with size-independent parameters, with no GNN and no memorization. It also prevents the per-layer overfitting the free (γ_ℓ, SNAP_ℓ) parameters allow.

**Cheapest experiment.** Use the winning driver from probe 1, q = 2 (4 parameters), trained at n9 L4. Sweep L at n12 and n15. Metric: Med/B90 versus L. You want it to keep falling monotonically.

### 4. Cost connectivity: split native intra-cavity phases from bus phases, with class-shared γ

**What to try.**
- Switch to K = 4 bits per cavity (the 16-level layout clean16 used). Some 4-clauses then lie inside one cavity and can be implemented exactly by SNAP, for free.
- Implement only the inter-cavity clauses through the bus or a cross-cavity phase.
- Use separate γ_ℓ^(span) for each span class s ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4}: 4 scalars per layer, no GNN.

**Why.** It makes the cost hardware-native and cheaper, while keeping every parameter size-independent. Unlike εΔ or GNN clause weights, it cannot memorize nonlocal phase. It only transfers if training covers all span classes, which is why it depends on probe 2.

**Cheapest experiment.** n8 and n12 (K = 4, C = 2 and 3), ideal-X or projector driver, L4. Compare a single γ_ℓ against span-split γ_ℓ^(s). Metric: n16 zero-shot Med/B90 against clean16's QAOA L4 (34.1/84.9).

### 5. Fix the seq-evolve vocabulary, then resume it

**What to try.** In `mutate_motif`, add compiled blocks as atomic ops: `W`, `W†`, `SNAP0(β)`, `GRAYX(β)` (a fitted SNAP+D chain), and `FLAT_PREP`. Initialize from probe 1's winner instead of N(0, 0.3), and promote on fitted b over 9/12/15 rather than on S at n12.

**Why.** Today's search is limited to layers of at most 6 raw ops with cold init. It cannot reach any known transfer-capable mixer. With compiled atoms, evolution explores *orderings and compositions of good drivers*, which is where native novelty could actually show up.

**Cheapest experiment.** 20 trials × 80 steps seeded from probe 1, training on {9, 12}, scored by b.

(If you ever return to the GNN: only let it emit a bounded residual, about tanh·0.1, around the fixed-angle base, and reject any candidate whose n12 result is worse than that base. That alone stops the 4e8 blow-up.)
