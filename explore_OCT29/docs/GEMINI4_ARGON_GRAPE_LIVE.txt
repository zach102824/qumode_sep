### Direct Response to Zach’s Belief
> *"GRAPE uses gradients to approximate, so we MUST need the (full) matrix to do that."*

**Why this conflates two different levels of compilation:**  
GRAPE indeed requires an explicit target matrix $U_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ and dense matrix propagators to compute forward/backward states and gradients—**on the Hilbert space of the gate being synthesized**. However, GRAPE is only ever run **offline on a local 1-, 2-, or $k$-mode gadget** (dimension $N \ll 2^n$), never on the global $n$-variable SAT instance. Once a finite library of local clause/entangler gadgets is pulse-compiled and frozen, the $n$-variable instance unitary $U_c(\gamma)$ is assembled purely as a **classical schedule (list of tuples: gate type, target modes, literal phases)** of $m$ local pulses. The global $2^n \times 2^n$ (or $d^n \times d^n$) matrix is never formed, differentiated, or stored.

---

### (A) Does standard GRAPE require the densified full-instance $U_c$ matrix of size $2^n \times 2^n$ (or $d^n \times d^n$)?

**NO.**

* **Why:** Standard GRAPE requires the dense matrix of the **target gate being optimized** ($N \times N$). In a modular / gadgetized compilation flow for $k$-SAT ($H_c = \sum_{c=1}^m C_c$), we do **not** pass the monolithic $n$-mode circuit $U_c(\gamma) = e^{-i\gamma H_c}$ into GRAPE. Instead:
  1. Because all clause projectors $C_c$ mutually commute ($[C_c, C_{c'}] = 0$), the global cost unitary factors **exactly** into a product of $m$ local $k$-mode clause unitaries:
     \[
     U_c(\gamma) = \exp\!\Bigl(-i\gamma \sum_{c=1}^m C_c\Bigr) = \prod_{c=1}^m e^{-i\gamma C_c} = \prod_{c=1}^m \Bigl[I + (e^{-i\gamma}-1)C_c\Bigr].
     \]
  2. Each clause unitary $U^{(c)}(\gamma) = I + (e^{-i\gamma}-1)C_c$ acts non-trivially on at most $k$ modes (or is further decomposed into $O(2^k)$ 1-mode and 2-mode entangling gates plus local literal-negation permutation unitaries).
  3. GRAPE is run **once offline** on the local 2-mode (or $k$-mode) subsystem of dimension $N = d_1 d_2$ (or $d^k$). At instance runtime, the compiler simply emits a classical schedule of $O(m)$ pre-compiled pulses routed to the clause indices $(i_{c,1}, \dots, i_{c,k})$.

---

### (B) Does the fact that GRAPE uses gradients force needing that full instance matrix?

**NO.**

* **What matrices the gradient actually uses:** To evaluate the objective $\Phi$ and its analytic gradient $\partial \Phi / \partial u_k(j)$ across $M$ time slices, GRAPE only allocates and multiplies matrices on the **local controlled subsystem** of dimension $N \times N$:
  1. **Slice propagators** $G_j = \exp\!\bigl(-i\Delta t\,[H_0 + \sum_k u_k(j) H_k]\bigr) \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ for $j = 1, \dots, M$.
  2. **Forward cumulative propagators** $U_j = G_j G_{j-1} \cdots G_1 \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ (from $t_0=0$ to $t_j$).
  3. **Backward / adjoint costate propagators** $\lambda_j = G_{j+1}^\dagger \cdots G_M^\dagger U_{\mathrm{tgt}} \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ (propagating the local target $U_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ backward from $t_M = T$ to $t_j$).
  4. **Control generators** $H_0, H_k \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$.
* **Dimension $N$:** $N$ is the truncated dimension of the **gadget subsystem** (e.g., $N = d_{\mathrm{cav}}^2$ for two bosonic modes, or $N = d_{\mathrm{cav}}^2 d_{\mathrm{transmon}}$ with a coupler), typically $N \in [16, 256]$. It is **completely independent of the number of SAT variables $n$ and clauses $m$**.

### (C) Detailed Usual GRAPE Procedure (Khaneja et al., *J. Magn. Reson.* 172, 296–305, 2005)

Consider synthesizing a target unitary $U_{\mathrm{tgt}} \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ over total duration $T$ partitioned into $M$ equal steps of width $\Delta t = T/M$.

#### 1. System Hamiltonian & Piecewise-Constant Controls
On the $N$-dimensional truncated Hilbert space of the local device subsystem, the Hamiltonian during time bin $j \in \{1, \dots, M\}$ ($t \in [(j-1)\Delta t, j\Delta t]$) is constant:
\[
H(t_j) = H_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{K} u_k(j)\, H_k,
\]
where $H_0 \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ is the drift Hamiltonian (e.g., Kerr/dispersive shifts), $H_k \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ are the $K$ control Hamiltonians (e.g., cavity/transmon $I/Q$ drives), and $u_k(j) \in \mathbb{R}$ are the $K \times M$ control amplitudes to be optimized.

#### 2. Forward Propagators $U_j$
Define the single-step propagator for slice $j$:
\[
G_j = \exp\!\bigl(-i\,\Delta t\, H(t_j)\bigr) \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}.
\]
The forward cumulative propagator from $t=0$ to $t_j = j\Delta t$ is built recursively:
\[
U_0 = I_N, \qquad U_j = G_j U_{j-1} = G_j G_{j-1} \cdots G_1 \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}, \qquad U_f \equiv U_M.
\]

#### 3. Fidelity Objective $\Phi$
To make the figure of merit invariant to global phase (Eq. 30–32 in Khaneja et al. 2005), define the normalized Hilbert–Schmidt inner product on $\mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$:
\[
\langle A \mid B \rangle = \frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}(A^\dagger B), \qquad \Phi = \bigl|\langle U_{\mathrm{tgt}} \mid U_f \rangle\bigr|^2 = \frac{1}{N^2}\bigl|\mathrm{Tr}(U_{\mathrm{tgt}}^\dagger U_f)\bigr|^2 \in [0, 1].
\]

#### 4. Backward / Adjoint Propagators $\lambda_j$
Using the cyclic property of the trace, the overlap at step $j$ splits into forward and backward parts:
\[
\mathrm{Tr}(U_{\mathrm{tgt}}^\dagger U_f) = \mathrm{Tr}\!\bigl(U_{\mathrm{tgt}}^\dagger G_M \cdots G_{j+1} G_j U_{j-1}\bigr) = \mathrm{Tr}(\lambda_j^\dagger U_j),
\]
where the adjoint (costate) matrix $\lambda_j \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}$ propagates $U_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ backward in time from $t_M$ to $t_j$:
\[
\lambda_M = U_{\mathrm{tgt}}, \qquad \lambda_{j-1} = G_j^\dagger \lambda_j = G_j^\dagger G_{j+1}^\dagger \cdots G_M^\dagger U_{\mathrm{tgt}} \in \mathbb{C}^{N \times N}.
\]

#### 5. Analytic Gradient $\partial \Phi / \partial u_k(j)$
Perturbing $u_k(j) \to u_k(j) + \delta u_k(j)$ only alters slice propagator $G_j$. To first order in $\Delta t$ (Khaneja et al. Eq. 12 & Eq. 33; or exactly via the Frechet derivative / eigenbasis commutator of $-i\Delta t H(t_j)$):
\[
\frac{\partial G_j}{\partial u_k(j)} \approx -i\,\Delta t\, H_k G_j \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \frac{\partial U_f}{\partial u_k(j)} \approx -i\,\Delta t\, G_M \cdots G_{j+1} H_k G_j \cdots G_1.
\]
Substituting into $\Phi = \frac{1}{N^2}\langle U_{\mathrm{tgt}} \mid U_f \rangle \langle U_f \mid U_{\mathrm{tgt}} \rangle$ yields the closed-form gradient for every $(k, j)$:
\[
\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial u_k(j)} = -\frac{2}{N^2}\,\mathrm{Im}\!\Bigl[\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\lambda_j^\dagger (i\,\Delta t\, H_k)\, U_j\bigr)\,\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(U_f^\dagger U_{\mathrm{tgt}}\bigr)\Bigr] = \frac{2\,\Delta t}{N^2}\,\mathrm{Im}\!\Bigl[\mathrm{Tr}(\lambda_j^\dagger H_k U_j)\,\mathrm{Tr}(U_f^\dagger U_{\mathrm{tgt}})\Bigr].
\]
*(Note: When evaluating the exact matrix-exponential derivative $\partial G_j/\partial u_k(j) = G_j \bar{H}_k$, one simply replaces $-i\Delta t H_k U_j$ by $(\partial G_j/\partial u_k(j))U_{j-1}$ at the same $O(N^3)$ per-slice cost.)*

#### 6. What is $N$?
$N$ is the dimension of the **truncated Hilbert space of the local physical gadget** being controlled (or $N_{\mathrm{comp}} \le N$ in the denominator if projecting fidelity onto the computational subspace while evolving $U_j, \lambda_j$ in an $N_{\mathrm{trunc}}$-dimensional buffer space to penalize leakage). $N$ is **never** the $2^n$ (or $d^n$) Hilbert space of the global $n$-variable SAT instance.

#### 7. Standard Algorithm Loop
1. **Initialize:** Choose initial control waveforms $u_k^{(0)}(j)$ ($k=1\dots K$, $j=1\dots M$) on the $N$-dimensional subsystem.
2. **Forward pass ($j = 1 \to M$):** Compute $G_j = \exp(-i\Delta t H(t_j))$ and store $U_j = G_j U_{j-1}$ ($M$ matrix exponentials and $M$ multiplications of size $N \times N$).
3. **Backward pass ($j = M \to 1$):** Set $\lambda_M = U_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ and recursively compute $\lambda_{j-1} = G_j^\dagger \lambda_j$ ($M$ multiplications of size $N \times N$).
4. **Gradient evaluation:** For each $j \in \{1,\dots,M\}$ and $k \in \{1,\dots,K\}$, compute $\partial \Phi / \partial u_k(j)$ via the trace inner product $\mathrm{Tr}(\lambda_j^\dagger H_k U_j)$ in $O(N^2)$ time per control channel.
5. **Update controls:** Take a quasi-Newton (L-BFGS-B) or conjugate-gradient step $u_k^{(r+1)}(j) = u_k^{(r)}(j) + \alpha_r d_k^{(r)}(j)$ (optionally adding bandwidth/amplitude/leakage penalty gradients).
6. **Terminate:** Repeat steps 2–5 until $1 - \Phi \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{gadget}}^2$. Save the frozen pulse array $\{u_k^*(j)\}$ to disk.

### (D) Confirm or Refute: Offline GRAPE of Local Gadgets + Classical Schedule Bypasses Forming Densified $2^n$ $U_c$

**YES (Confirmed)** — under the stated caveats below.

Synthesizing a universal 2-mode entangler (or direct $k$-mode clause projector unitary) on a truncated Fock space via GRAPE and then compiling an $n$-variable, $m$-clause $k$-SAT instance as a classical schedule of those frozen pulses **completely bypasses** forming, storing, or differentiating the $2^n \times 2^n$ (or $d^n \times d^n$) instance matrix $U_c$.

---

### (E) Detailed Explanation of the Caveats

1. **Commuting Clause Terms (Why Product = Exponential Without Trotter Error):**
   * Each clause $c$ penalizes a single falsifying $k$-bit assignment via a diagonal Fock/computational projector $C_c = \bigotimes_{r=1}^k P_{i_{c,r}}^{(\ell_{c,r})}$ with $C_c^2 = C_c$ and $C_c^\dagger = C_c$.
   * Because every $C_c$ is diagonal in the same global Fock/computational basis, all clause operators commute pairwise: $[C_c, C_{c'}] = 0$ for all $c, c'$.
   * By the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff / Zassenhaus identity, vanishing commutators make the first-order product formula **exact** for any angle $\gamma$:
     \[
     \exp(-i\gamma H_c) = \exp\!\Bigl(-i\gamma \sum_{c=1}^m C_c\Bigr) = \prod_{c=1}^m \exp(-i\gamma C_c) = \prod_{c=1}^m \Bigl[I + (e^{-i\gamma}-1)C_c\Bigr].
     \]
   * **Consequence:** There is **zero Trotterization error** between clauses, and clauses can be reordered arbitrarily (e.g., via edge/hypergraph coloring to execute disjoint clauses in parallel).

2. **Fixed Locality $k$ (Gadget Cost $\sim d^k$ or $O(2^k)$ Two-Mode Entanglers):**
   * Because $k$ is fixed (e.g., $k=3$ or $k=4$) and does not grow with $n$, each clause unitary acts on a constant-size Hilbert space.
   * **Option 1 (Direct $k$-mode gadget):** GRAPE optimizes the $k$-mode diagonal phase gate directly on a truncated space of size $N = d_{\mathrm{trunc}}^k = O(1)$ with respect to $n$, conjugated by single-mode literal-negation flips ($X_i$ for positive/negative literals).
   * **Option 2 (2-mode decomposition):** The $k$-controlled phase gate $I + (e^{-i\gamma}-1)C_c$ is decomposed exactly into $O(2^k)$ two-mode diagonal entanglers (e.g., CSWAP/CZ/SNAP-type or Walsh–Hadamard parity rotations) and 1-mode gates. GRAPE only ever compiles 1-mode ($N = d_{\mathrm{trunc}}$) and 2-mode ($N = d_{\mathrm{trunc}}^2$) primitives.
   * If $k$ scaled as $\Theta(n)$, either $N = d^k$ or the decomposition length $O(2^k)$ would blow up exponentially in $n$; fixed $k = O(1)$ guarantees per-clause compilation cost is $O(1)$ in $n$.

3. **Error Budget ($m \cdot \varepsilon_{\mathrm{gadget}}$, Leakage/Truncation, and Spectators):**
   * **Additive operator-norm error accumulation:** By the telescope/triangle inequality for unitary products, if each compiled clause gadget $\tilde{U}^{(c)}$ approximates the ideal $U^{(c)}$ on the computational subspace within diamond/operator norm error $\bigl\|\tilde{U}^{(c)} - U^{(c)}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{gadget}}$, then the $m$-clause schedule satisfies
     \[
     \Bigl\|\prod_{c=1}^m \tilde{U}^{(c)} - U_c(\gamma)\Bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \sum_{c=1}^m \bigl\|\tilde{U}^{(c)} - U^{(c)}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le m\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gadget}}.
     \]
     Thus, to achieve total circuit error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{total}}$ across $p$ QAOA/TEB layers, offline GRAPE must hit $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gadget}} \lesssim \varepsilon_{\mathrm{total}} / (p m)$ (or $\sim \varepsilon_{\mathrm{total}} / (\sqrt{pm})$ for incoherent/unbiased phase errors).
   * **Fock truncation & leakage:** Bosonic cavities have infinite Hilbert spaces. GRAPE optimizes on a truncated Fock cutoff $d_{\mathrm{trunc}} > d_{\mathrm{comp}}$ (including guard levels) with a leakage penalty so population stays inside the computational subspace; any residual leakage $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leak}}$ per gadget also accumulates across the $m$ clauses.
   * **Idle spectators & crosstalk:** While a pulse acts on the $k$ modes of clause $c$, the remaining $n - k$ spectator modes are assumed to evolve as $I^{\otimes (n-k)}$. In hardware, always-on dispersive/cross-Kerr couplings ($\chi_{ij} \hat{n}_i \hat{n}_j$) between active and spectator modes or self-Kerr drift on idle modes must be echoed out (dynamical decoupling), cancelled in the rotating frame, or bounded so spectator phase errors over total schedule duration $T_{\mathrm{sched}}$ remain below budget.
